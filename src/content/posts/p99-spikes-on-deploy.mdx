Here's a graph every team eventually stares at: you ship a deploy, the
<Annotate client:visible type="underline">p50 latency</Annotate> barely twitches, and the
**p99 doubles** for a few minutes before settling. Nothing about the model changed. So what
got slow?

## The tail is a different population

The median answers "how fast is a typical request." The tail answers "how fast is the
*unluckiest* request." During a rollout those are two different worlds:

- Most requests still land on warm replicas → p50 is fine.
- A few land on a replica that just started → cold weights, cold cache, a
  <Annotate client:visible type="box" token="--hot">40-second</Annotate> first request.

You only need a small fraction of requests to hit a cold replica to move the 99th
percentile hard, while leaving the 50th untouched. The tail *is* the deploy.

<MarginNote>
  <b>Rule of thumb:</b> if p50 is flat and p99 spikes on a time-bounded event (deploy,
  scale-up, node replacement), suspect a cold or saturated subset of replicas — not the
  model.
</MarginNote>

## The load balancer makes it worse

A plain round-robin balancer treats the cold replica as equal and keeps handing it traffic
while it crawls. Three things take the edge off:

1. **Readiness that means ready.** Don't mark a replica healthy until it has loaded weights
   *and* served a warm-up request. A passing `/health` that returns before the model is
   loaded is how cold replicas get traffic.
2. **Least-outstanding-requests routing.** Send each request to the replica with the fewest
   in flight, so a slow one naturally sheds load instead of accumulating it.
3. **Request hedging.** For read-only inference, fire a backup request after a short delay
   and take whichever returns first. It trims the tail at the cost of a little extra work.

## What to watch

Put p50 and p99 on the *same* chart, mark your deploys on it, and add replica age. Once you
can see the tail track the rollout, the fix stops being "make the model faster" and becomes
"stop routing to cold replicas" — which is a much easier problem.
